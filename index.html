The Why, How, and What of Making Online Images Accessible

A recent review of Division of Student Affairs webpages found a lack of alt text on images. Alt text is a description added to an image to explain its content. It is the first step to web accessibility, and here is your quick guide to using it.

Poor alt text: "IUPUI Jaguars know how to have fun on the dance floor."

Good alt text: "Chancellor Paydar and a student dance on a colorful dance floor at IUPUI Birthday Bash."

Why is alt text important?

We get it, it can be tough to keep track of every best practice in the digital space. But alt text pays off in a few concrete ways.

Accessibility
Alt text lets your website communicate with people who have disabilities or limited vision by describing images through a screen reader. Skipping it creates a barrier for anyone who cannot see the page.
Search engine optimization
Search engines like Google scan pages for matching words. Describing an image with alt text helps search engines understand your content and surface your page in relevant searches.
Connection issues
Whether someone has images turned off or is on a slow connection, alt text is what appears in place of an image that fails to load.

How do you use alt text effectively?

Not every attempt at alt text is effective. Keep these practices in mind.

  • Describe what is shown in the photo itself. Avoid adding extra context the way a caption might.
  • Enter alt text for every image, except purely decorative ones that add nothing to the surrounding text.
  • Keep it concise. Aim for no more than 10 to 15 words.
  • Include any words that actually appear within an informative image.

What is expected of you?

The Division of Student Affairs is committed to making all of our content accessible. Do your part to uphold this commitment, and include alt text on every image you publish to the website.
